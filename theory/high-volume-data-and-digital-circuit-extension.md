# High-volume data and digital-circuit extension

## 0. What this document is

This is the design document for a second track of the project: a
**synthetic digital-circuit trace factory** that produces \(10^4\) to \(10^6\)
labeled traces from RTL simulation, plus the **statistical and probabilistic
machinery** needed to analyze that many traces without fooling ourselves.

It exists because the original plan can only produce a few hundred to a few
thousand hand-made synthetic traces before the bench stage, and because the
analysis methods in `model-and-proof-roadmap.md` (mutual information, payoff
matrix estimation, equilibrium confidence) all get sharper with large \(n\),
but only if the data generating process is honest and the statistics are
designed before the data arrives.

It also deliberately pulls the project toward **digital circuit and IC skills**
(RTL microarchitecture, clock gating, synthesis, timing/power reports, FPGA
prototyping) while keeping the RF/mixed-signal measurement story intact. The
digital side generates *switching activity*; the mixed-signal side turns
switching activity into an *observable trace*; the game theory decides *what to
switch and when*.

### The single most important caveat

> Switching activity extracted from a VCD/SAIF file is **not** silicon EM
> leakage. It is a **proxy** for the current demand of a digital block.
> Every claim in this track must be written as "under the declared activity
> proxy and channel model," not as "measured leakage." Calibration against real
> measurements is a later stage gate (R4 / Stage 8), and until that gate passes,
> all numbers are simulation numbers.

This is written to be read after a break, so each section starts with a short
refresher before the technical content. Skip the refreshers if they are stale
knowledge rather than forgotten knowledge.

### Relationship to the rest of the repository

- `research-project-master-plan.txt`: the stage structure this track plugs
  into (new Stage 2D; see that file).
- `theory/model-and-proof-roadmap.md`: the notation \(Y=H_aL(S,X)+B_d+N_d+W\),

## 1. Refresher: the digital half

### 1.1 Where a power/EM signature comes from

A CMOS gate burns energy mostly when it *switches*. For a node with capacitance
\(C\), supply \(V_{DD}\), switching at frequency \(f\) with activity factor
\(\alpha\) (expected transitions per clock),

\[
P_{\mathrm{dyn}} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,\alpha\, C\, V_{DD}^2\, f ,
\]

plus short-circuit and leakage terms that we treat as a slowly varying offset.
The practical consequence: **the number and identity of nets that toggle in a
clock cycle is the first-order determinant of the current drawn in that cycle.**
That is exactly what a VCD file records.

If \(\mathcal N\) is the set of nets, \(c_n\) a capacitance weight, and
\(\tau_n[k]\in\{0,1,2,\dots\}\) the number of transitions of net \(n\) during
cycle \(k\), the **per-cycle switched-capacitance proxy** is

\[
\boxed{\;Q[k] \;=\; \sum_{n\in\mathcal N} c_n\,\tau_n[k]\;}
\tag{1.1}
\]

with units of capacitance (F) if \(c_n\) are real capacitances, or just "toggle
count" if \(c_n=1\). Equation (1.1) is the bridge between RTL simulation and the
trace model. It is a proxy, not a measurement (§0).

### 1.2 Hamming weight and Hamming distance

Most published leakage models reduce (1.1) to a register-level summary. For a
\(b\)-bit register with value \(v\),

\[
\mathrm{HW}(v)=\sum_{i=0}^{b-1} v_i ,
\qquad
\mathrm{HD}(v,v')=\mathrm{HW}(v\oplus v')=\sum_{i=0}^{b-1}\mathbf 1[v_i\ne v'_i].
\]

- **HW model**: current \(\propto\) number of ones held (reasonable for
  precharged buses).
- **HD model**: current \(\propto\) number of bits that flipped between
  consecutive states (reasonable for CMOS registers; usually the better model).

For AES, the classic target intermediate is the S-box output of the first
round for key byte \(j\):

\[
Z_j = \mathrm{Sbox}\!\left(X_j \oplus K_j\right),
\qquad
L_j = \mathrm{HD}\!\left(Z_j,\,X_j\oplus K_j\right)
\quad\text{or}\quad
L_j=\mathrm{HW}(Z_j).
\tag{1.2}
\]

This is the *label* that makes a trace dataset supervised. In this project
\(K_j\) is always a synthetic test key generated by our own harness.

### 1.3 What RTL, netlist, and gate-level mean here

| Level | What it is | What switching activity means | Fidelity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Behavioral RTL | `always_ff` blocks, arithmetic operators | Register and declared-signal toggles only | Lowest; no glitches |
| Gate-level (post-synthesis, zero-delay) | Standard cells after Yosys/ABC mapping | All internal cell nets toggle | Medium; still no glitch timing |
| Gate-level with SDF timing | Netlist + cell/interconnect delays | Includes **glitches** (multiple toggles per cycle) | Highest available pre-silicon |

Glitch power is a large fraction of real dynamic power in arithmetic logic, and
glitch behavior is precisely what clock gating and dummy switching perturb. So
the staged ladder in §14 moves from behavioral RTL (fast, millions of traces)
to gate-level with timing (slow, tens of thousands of traces) and treats the
difference between them as a measured quantity, not an assumption.

### 1.4 Clock gating, DVFS, frequency hopping, dummy switching

These are the **defender actions** of the game, expressed in digital hardware:

- **Clock gating**: an `ICG` cell (integrated clock gate) that suppresses the
  clock to a register bank when its enable is low. Reduces \(\alpha\) to ~0 for
  that bank. Defense use: *randomly* gate non-critical banks to decorrelate
  activity from data, at a latency cost if the bank was needed.
- **DVFS / frequency hopping**: change \(f\) (and ideally \(V_{DD}\)) per
  operation or per block of cycles. Smears the clock fundamental and its
  harmonics across a band, which is why the RF observer's band choice matters.
- **Dummy switching block**: a register bank + combinational tree clocked
  deliberately to add \(\alpha\) uncorrelated with the secret, i.e. the
  \(N_d\) term. Costs energy, not latency.
- **Shuffling**: permute the order of independent S-box lookups. Spreads the
  secret-dependent event over a window of cycles.

Each maps onto a defender action \(d\in\mathcal D\) in
`model-and-proof-roadmap.md` §2, and each has an energy/latency price that the
synthesis and power reports will quantify (§13.6) rather than guess.

## 2. Refresher: the statistics half

Four ideas carry most of the weight in this document.

**(a) An effect estimate with an interval beats a p-value.** Report
\(\hat\delta \pm\) 95% CI. A p-value answers "could noise alone do this," which
is almost always "no" when \(n=10^6\); the interval answers "how big is it,"
which is the question that changes a design.

**(b) Correlated samples are not independent samples.** 1000 traces from one
simulation session with one netlist and one seed are *not* 1000 independent
observations of "the defense." The unit of independence is the **session /
netlist / seed group**, not the trace. All intervals must be computed with
grouping respected (cluster bootstrap, §8.3, or a hierarchical model, §8.7).
Getting this wrong is the single most common way a large side-channel dataset
produces a confidently wrong number.

**(c) Searching many hypotheses inflates false positives.** Scanning 2000 time
samples × 16 key bytes × 8 bands is 256,000 tests. At \(\alpha=0.05\) that is
~12,800 false alarms by construction. Use Holm (family-wise) or
Benjamini–Hochberg (false discovery rate); see §8.5.

**(d) Peeking at accumulating data invalidates fixed-\(n\) tests.** If we run
until the t-statistic crosses 4.5 and then stop, we will always cross it
eventually. Either fix \(n\) in advance, or use an **anytime-valid** procedure
(§10).

## 3. The design under test (DUT)

Two RTL targets, in order.

### 3.1 DUT-A: synthetic switching kernel (first, mandatory)

A deliberately simple, fully understood block whose leakage is *known by
construction*. Purpose: validate the entire pipeline and every estimator
against ground truth before any cryptographic kernel is involved.

```systemverilog
// Synthetic secret-dependent switching kernel (SystemVerilog, behavioral).
// Purpose: produce a controllable, known-ground-truth activity signature.
module synth_kernel #(
    parameter int WIDTH      = 32,
    parameter int DUMMY_BANK = 32
) (
    input  logic                 clk,
    input  logic                 rst_n,
    input  logic                 start,
    input  logic [WIDTH-1:0]     plaintext,   // public X
    input  logic [WIDTH-1:0]     secret,      // synthetic S (test-only)
    // defense controls (defender action d)
    input  logic                 cg_en,       // clock-gate non-critical bank
    input  logic [1:0]           dummy_level, // 0..3 dummy activity level
    input  logic [15:0]          prng_seed,
    output logic [WIDTH-1:0]     result,
    output logic                 done
);
  // secret-dependent datapath: the intended L(S,X)
  logic [WIDTH-1:0] mixed_q;
  always_ff @(posedge clk or negedge rst_n)
    if (!rst_n)       mixed_q <= '0;
    else if (start)   mixed_q <= plaintext ^ secret;   // HD target lives here

  // dummy bank: the intended N_d, clocked from an LFSR, never feeds result
  logic [DUMMY_BANK-1:0] dummy_q;
  logic [DUMMY_BANK-1:0] lfsr_bits;
  always_ff @(posedge clk or negedge rst_n)
    if (!rst_n)  dummy_q <= '0;
    else         dummy_q <= dummy_q ^ (lfsr_bits & dummy_mask(dummy_level));
  // ... ICG instance gated by cg_en for the non-critical bank ...
endmodule
```

Ground truth we can check: the HD of `mixed_q` is exactly
\(\mathrm{HW}(X\oplus S\oplus \text{prev})\), so the correlation between the
toggle-count feature and the HD label must be ~1 when `dummy_level=0`, and must
fall as `dummy_level` rises in a predictable way. If the pipeline cannot
recover that, the pipeline is broken.

### 3.2 DUT-B: small AES core (second)

A byte-serial or 32-bit-datapath AES-128 encryption core, either written
in-house or taken from a permissively licensed open core (e.g. the `tiny-aes`
style designs on OpenCores / GitHub). **The license must be recorded in the dataset
manifest.** Only the first-round S-box output is used as a label, per (1.2).

Requirements:
- Key and plaintext driven from the testbench; **synthetic keys only**.
- A `trigger` output asserted for the window of interest, mirroring the GPIO
  trigger that the Stage 8 bench setup will use. Keeping the simulated and
  measured trigger semantics identical is what makes R4 calibration possible.
- The same defense control ports as DUT-A, so a defender action means the same
  thing on both DUTs.

### 3.3 Defense wrapper (shared)

A `defense_wrapper` module instantiates the DUT plus:
- an ICG-based clock-gating controller,
- a clock-mode selector (frequency hopping, §13.3),
- the dummy switching bank,
- an LFSR or CSPRNG as the randomness source (§13.4),
- a register map exposing mode/level/seed (§13.8).

The defender action \(d\) is literally a write to that register map. This is the
design decision that keeps the game theory and the hardware honest: if an
action cannot be expressed as a register write, it is not an action.

## 4. Toolchain

| Need | Tool | Why | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| RTL simulation, behavioral | **Icarus Verilog (`iverilog`)** | Trivial setup, good SystemVerilog-subset support, writes VCD natively | Free |
| RTL simulation, fast/large | **Verilator** | Compiles to C++; 10–100× faster; needed for \(10^5\)–\(10^6\) traces | Free |
| Waveform inspection | GTKWave / Surfer | Debug only, never in the analysis path | Free |
| Synthesis to gates | **Yosys** | Open-source; maps RTL to a standard-cell library | Free |
| Standard-cell library | **SkyWater Sky130 PDK** | Real cell capacitances and timing; makes \(c_n\) physical | Free |
| Full RTL-to-GDS (optional, later) | **OpenLane / OpenROAD** | Placement/routing gives interconnect parasitics and real power reports | Free |
| Power estimation | Yosys/OpenSTA + SAIF, or own (1.1) | Cross-check our proxy against a tool's | Free |
| Assertions | SystemVerilog assertions in iverilog/Verilator | Prove the defense controller never violates protocol | Free |
| FPGA prototype (optional, R4) | Vivado / Quartus / Yosys+nextpnr | Real silicon switching, real supply current | Board cost only |

**Decision: Icarus first, Verilator for scale.** Write the testbench so the
same DUT compiles under both. Icarus is the reference (easier assertions, VCD
for free); Verilator is the production generator with a C++ harness that writes
features directly and only optionally dumps waveforms. Mismatches between the
two simulators on the same seed are a bug, and a regression test should check
that per-cycle toggle counts agree exactly at the behavioral level.

### 4.1 VCD versus SAIF

- **VCD** (Value Change Dump) is a full event log: every net, every transition,
  with timestamps. It gives per-cycle resolution, which we need because the
  trace is a time series. It is enormous: a few hundred MB per thousand traces
  if dumped naively.
- **SAIF** (Switching Activity Interchange Format) is an *aggregate*: toggle
  counts and static probabilities per net over a window. Much smaller, but it
  destroys time resolution, so it cannot produce a trace.

**Decision: VCD for trace generation, SAIF only for whole-run power sanity
checks and for feeding a synthesis power report.** Mitigate VCD size by
(a) restricting `$dumpvars` to the DUT scope and a declared net list,
(b) dumping only the trigger window, (c) parsing VCD to features in a streaming
fashion and **deleting the VCD immediately**, keeping only the per-cycle vector
\(Q[k]\) and the features. The raw VCD is reproducible from the seed, so it is
not data worth storing.

At scale, the better path is to skip VCD entirely: a Verilator C++ harness can
compute (1.1) directly in the simulation loop by watching the signals of
interest. Keep the VCD path as the slow, auditable reference and cross-check it
against the fast path on a fixed seed set.

## 5. From switching activity to an observable trace

### 5.1 The channel model

Per-cycle activity \(Q[k]\) from (1.1) is upsampled to a sample-rate time
series and pushed through a mixed-signal channel. With samples per cycle
\(M=f_s/f_{\mathrm{clk}}\),

\[
i[n] \;=\; \sum_{k} Q[k]\,g\!\left(n - kM - \phi_k\right),
\tag{5.1}
\]

where \(g(\cdot)\) is a **current pulse shape** (a normalized unit-area pulse of
width ~a few gate delays) and \(\phi_k\) is a per-cycle jitter/offset term that
frequency hopping and clock jitter modulate. Then

\[
\boxed{\;
y[n] \;=\; \alpha(a)\,\big(h_{\mathrm{PDN}} * i\big)[n]
\;+\;\big(h_{\mathrm{PDN}} * i_{\mathrm{dummy}}\big)[n]
\;+\; w[n] \;}
\tag{5.2}
\]

which is exactly \(Y=H_aL(S,X)+B_d+N_d+W\) from the proof roadmap, with the
pieces now *derived from RTL* instead of invented:

| Roadmap symbol | Source in this pipeline |
|---|---|
| \(L(S,X)\) | \(Q[k]\) restricted to secret-dependent nets, via (5.1) |
| \(B_d\) | deterministic defense activity (clock-gating pattern, mode switch transients) |
| \(N_d\) | dummy bank activity \(i_{\mathrm{dummy}}\), driven by the LFSR/CSPRNG |
| \(H_a\) | observer's band-pass/probe response \(\alpha(a)\), applied as a filter |
| \(W\) | \(w[n]\): thermal + quantization + ambient, with declared PSD |

\(h_{\mathrm{PDN}}\) is a power-delivery-network impedance model: a 2nd or 3rd
order \(RLC\) with a declared resonance, ideally fitted later to an LTspice
model or a measured \(Z_{\mathrm{PDN}}(f)\). **Until it is fitted, it is a
declared assumption and must appear in every figure caption.**

For the RF/screaming-channel variant, add a multiplicative mixing term with a
carrier \(\cos(2\pi f_c n/f_s)\) so that the activity appears as sidebands
around \(f_c\); this is what makes band selection a real observer decision. The
mixing model is a cartoon of the real coupling mechanism and is labeled as such.

### 5.2 Why this is still useful despite being a proxy

1. It is **internally consistent**: label, activity, and trace come from the
   same simulation, so a recovered correlation is not an artifact of two
   independent random processes.
2. It is **controllable**: we can set the true leakage to zero and confirm the
   estimators report zero (null calibration, §8.6).
3. It is **cheap at scale**, which is the entire point of this track.
4. It produces an **ordering** of defenses. Even if absolute leakage is wrong by
   an order of magnitude, if D3 beats D1 under the proxy and the ordering
   survives the gate-level and FPGA gates, the ordering is the publishable
   object.

What it is *not*: evidence about absolute attack feasibility on real silicon.

### 6.1 Scale targets

| Phase | Traces | DUT / level | Purpose | Est. wall time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P0 | 1k | DUT-A behavioral | pipeline smoke test, ground-truth check | minutes |
| P1 | **10k** | DUT-A + DUT-B behavioral | first real statistics, all estimators exercised | ~1 h |
| P2 | 50k | DUT-B behavioral, 4 defenses × 4 observers | first payoff matrix with CIs | ~hours |
| P3 | **100k–1M** | DUT-B behavioral, full design grid | hierarchical model, MI convergence, power analysis | ~1 day on 8 cores |

| P5 | 10k+ | FPGA capture (optional) | calibrate the proxy | gated |

The \(\ge\)10k target at P1 is the one that must be met before any statistical
claim; P3 is the scalability demonstration. Note that P3 is *not* just "more of

mutual information in regimes where the bias correction matters (§8.6, §9.3).

### 6.2 Storage format

Three distinct data products with different access patterns:

| Product | Shape | Access pattern | Format |
|---|---|---|---|
| Raw traces | \(N\times T\) float32/int16 | slice by trace ranges, stream sequentially | **Zarr** (primary) / HDF5 (alt) |
| Per-trace features + labels + metadata | \(N\times F\) mixed types | filter, group-by, join, aggregate | **Parquet** |
| Run manifests | small JSON | read once | JSON + checksum |

**Recommendation: Zarr for raw arrays, Parquet for the feature/label table.**

Rationale:

- **Parquet** is columnar: `SELECT mean(feature) WHERE defense='D3' AND
  split='test'` reads only the needed columns, compresses integer label columns
  to almost nothing, and is directly queryable by pandas/pyarrow/DuckDB/Polars
  without a loader. It is *wrong* for a \(10^6\times 2000\) float array (row
  groups become huge, and "give me traces 500k–501k" is not a columnar query),
  which is why raw traces go elsewhere.
- **Zarr** stores the array as independent compressed chunks (e.g.
  \(1000\times 2000\)) as separate objects. Independent chunks mean **many
  worker processes can write different chunk ranges concurrently without a
  lock**. That is exactly the pattern of a parallel simulation farm. `dask`/
  `xarray` can map over it out-of-core. It also moves to object storage
  unchanged if the dataset outgrows the laptop.
- **HDF5** is the alternative: single file (tidy), excellent tooling, but the
  classic C library is effectively single-writer and parallel HDF5 needs an MPI
  build. If generation is single-process, HDF5 is fine and arguably simpler;
  if generation is a process pool, Zarr avoids a whole class of corruption bugs.
- Rejected: **NPY/NPZ** (no chunking, no compression control, whole-array
  loads), **CSV** (catastrophic at this scale: ~20× larger and slow to parse),
  **pickle** (not an archival format; version-fragile).

Sizing, \(T=2000\) samples:

\[
\text{bytes} \approx N\cdot T\cdot s,\qquad
\begin{cases}
N=10^4,\ s=4: & 80\ \mathrm{MB}\\
N=10^5,\ s=4: & 800\ \mathrm{MB}\\
N=10^6,\ s=4: & 8\ \mathrm{GB}\\
N=10^6,\ s=2\ (\text{int16}): & 4\ \mathrm{GB}
\end{cases}
\]

With Blosc/Zstd compression on smooth traces, expect 2–4×, so \(10^6\) traces
land at 1–4 GB, comfortable on a laptop SSD. **Store int16 with a recorded
scale factor** when the channel model's dynamic range allows it; it halves
everything and mirrors a real 12–14 bit ADC. Record the quantization in the
manifest, because quantization noise is part of \(W\).

Chunking rule: chunk along traces, never split a single trace across chunks.
Chunk size ~8–64 MB uncompressed.

### 6.4 Reproducible seeds

A single 64-bit `master_seed` plus a deterministic derivation:

\[
\texttt{seed}(\text{role}, \text{indices})
= \mathrm{BLAKE2b}\big(\texttt{master\_seed} \,\|\, \text{role} \,\|\,
\text{indices}\big) \bmod 2^{64}.
\]

Use `numpy.random.SeedSequence(master_seed).spawn(...)` in practice; it is a
tested implementation of exactly this idea. Separate streams for simulation,
noise, defense randomness, plaintexts, and key generation, so that **changing
the noise realization does not change the plaintexts**. That independence is
what makes "same data, different noise" and "same noise, different data"
ablations possible, and it is worth the small amount of plumbing.

Record: `master_seed`, generator git commit, tool versions (`iverilog -V`,
`verilator --version`, Yosys, PDK tag), OS, and a SHA-256 of every output file.

### 6.5 Splits: by group, never by trace

Random per-trace splitting is **wrong** here and will silently inflate every
result. Traces from the same session share a netlist, a PVT corner, a noise
process, and often a key. A model or estimator that sees session 7 in training
has effectively seen session 7's test traces.

Split hierarchy, outermost first:

1. **Key** (`key_id`): test keys never appear in train/calib. Mandatory for any
   key-recovery claim.
2. **Session** (`session_id`): a session is one contiguous simulation run with
   one RNG stream; mirrors a bench capture session.
3. **Netlist** (`netlist_id`): different synthesis runs / `-seed` values produce
   genuinely different gate-level structure. Holding out netlists tests whether
   a conclusion is about *the defense* or about *one synthesis outcome*.
4. **PVT corner**: hold out at least one corner entirely (e.g. train on `tt`,
   test on `ss`) to test robustness to process/voltage/temperature.

Target proportions: 60% train (attack/model fitting), 20% calibration
(observer band selection, threshold setting, estimator bias calibration), 20%
test (confirmatory, touched once). Implement with `GroupShuffleSplit` /
`GroupKFold` on the composite group key, and **assert in code** that the
intersection of group sets across splits is empty. That assertion is a test, not
a comment.

## 7. Features

Features are computed once at generation time and stored; the analysis never
touches raw traces except for plots and for re-deriving a feature under a new
definition (which creates a new dataset version).

### 7.1 Activity-domain (digital) features

- `toggle_total`: \(\sum_k Q[k]\), \(c_n=1\).
- `switched_cap_total`: \(\sum_k Q[k]\) with Sky130 pin capacitances.
- Per-cycle vector \(\{Q[k]\}_{k=1}^{P}\): the highest-resolution activity
  feature, and the one that makes "which cycle leaks" answerable.
- Per-block decomposition \(Q_\beta[k]\) for \(\beta\in\{\text{sbox},
  \text{mixcol}, \text{keysched}, \text{dummy}, \text{ctrl}\}\). This is a
  **microarchitectural attribution** and is the feature that turns a leakage
  result into a design change.
- `label_hw`, `label_hd` from (1.2).

### 7.2 Time-domain trace features

- Mean, variance, and max of \(y[n]\) in the trigger window.
- Windowed means \(\bar y_m = \frac{1}{|W_m|}\sum_{n\in W_m} y[n]\) over
  contiguous windows, to reduce 2000 samples to ~20–50 tests and cut the
  multiple-comparison burden before it starts.
- Peak sample index (alignment diagnostic; misalignment destroys leakage and
  must be distinguished from a working defense).

### 7.3 Spectral features

Welch PSD with declared window, segment length \(L\), overlap, and detrend:

\[
\hat S_{yy}(f) = \frac{1}{K}\sum_{\kappa=1}^{K}
\frac{1}{L U f_s}\left|\sum_{n=0}^{L-1} w[n]\,y_\kappa[n]\,
e^{-j2\pi f n/f_s}\right|^2,
\qquad U=\frac{1}{L}\sum_n w[n]^2 .
\]

Band energies over bands \(\mathcal B_b\) (clock fundamental, 2nd/3rd harmonic,
baseband, the hop span, and an off-band control):

\[
E_b = \int_{\mathcal B_b}\hat S_{yy}(f)\,df .
\]

**Include an off-band control band.** If a defense "reduces leakage" in the
control band too, the effect is gain or alignment, not the defense.

### 7.4 Observer-side preprocessing

The observer action \(a\) is applied as a feature transform: band-pass to
\(\mathcal B_a\), optional high-pass/differencing, optional trace averaging over
a budget \(n_a\). Averaging \(n\) traces with the same plaintext improves SNR by
\(\sqrt n\). That is exactly why trace budget is an observer action with a
cost \(c_{\text{trace}}\), and why the game is not trivially won by the
defender.

## 8. Statistical and probabilistic analysis

### 8.1 Hypotheses, declared before the data

Primary metric for every confirmatory comparison is fixed in advance.

| ID | Null \(H_0\) | Alternative \(H_1\) | Primary metric | Data |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | Toggle-count feature is independent of `label_hd` under D0 | Positive association | Pearson \(\rho\) | P1 test split |
| H2 | D3 and D0 have equal leakage | D3 strictly lower | \(\Delta\hat I = \hat I_{D0}-\hat I_{D3}\) (bits) | P2/P3 test split |
| H3 | D3's advantage survives the adaptive observer | D3 advantage shrinks to 0 under \(a^\star\) | \(\Delta\hat I\) at observer best response | P2/P3 test |
| H4 | Behavioral and gate-level orderings of \(\mathcal D\) agree | Ordering changes | Kendall \(\tau\) between orderings | P1 vs P4 |
| H5 | Equilibrium policy \(p^\star\) equals the robust-optimization policy | They differ | \(\|p^\star-p_{\text{robust}}\|_1\) | P3 payoff matrix |
| H6 | Defense effect is homogeneous across netlists/PVT | Heterogeneous | Hierarchical \(\tau^2 > 0\) | P3/P4 |

H3 and H5 are the ones that decide whether the game framing earns its place
(master plan §7). H6 is the one that decides whether a single number can even
be reported. Note that H2's alternative is one-sided and H4's null is the
*agreement*, an unusual direction that we want to *fail to reject*, so H4
needs an equivalence framing (TOST) rather than a significance test; see §9.4.

### 8.2 Welch t / TVLA, and its limits

For groups \(0,1\) (fixed vs. random plaintext) at time sample \(n\),

\[
t_n=\frac{\bar y^{(0)}_n-\bar y^{(1)}_n}
{\sqrt{s^2_{0,n}/N_0+s^2_{1,n}/N_1}},
\qquad
\nu_n=\frac{\left(s^2_{0,n}/N_0+s^2_{1,n}/N_1\right)^2}
{\dfrac{(s^2_{0,n}/N_0)^2}{N_0-1}+\dfrac{(s^2_{1,n}/N_1)^2}{N_1-1}} .
\]

Report **Cohen's d** alongside, since \(|t|\) grows with \(\sqrt N\) and is
therefore not comparable across phases:

\[
d_n=\frac{\bar y^{(0)}_n-\bar y^{(1)}_n}{s_{p,n}},\qquad
s_{p,n}^2=\frac{(N_0-1)s_{0,n}^2+(N_1-1)s_{1,n}^2}{N_0+N_1-2}.
\]

The \(|t|>4.5\) convention corresponds to a Bonferroni-ish threshold for
~\(10^5\) tests at \(\alpha=10^{-5}\). At \(N=10^6\) it is *far* too permissive
as a security statement and *far* too strict as a detection statement. Use it as
a screening convention only, exactly as the master plan says, and always
alongside \(d\) and its CI.

### 8.3 Cluster bootstrap for confidence intervals

Because traces within a session are correlated, resample **sessions**, not
traces.

Let \(G\) be the number of sessions and \(\hat\delta\) the statistic. For
\(b=1..B\) (use \(B\ge 2000\) for a 95% interval, \(B\ge 10^4\) if reporting
tail quantiles):

1. Draw \(G\) session IDs with replacement.
2. Pool all traces from the drawn sessions (a session drawn twice contributes
   twice).
3. Recompute \(\hat\delta^{*b}\) **from scratch**, including any
   standardization, band selection, or threshold fitting. Re-fitting inside the
   bootstrap loop is what makes the interval honest; fitting outside and only
   resampling the final numbers understates the width, sometimes badly.

Percentile interval: \(\left[\hat\delta^{*}_{(0.025B)},
\hat\delta^{*}_{(0.975B)}\right]\). Prefer **BCa** when \(\hat\delta\) is
skewed, which mutual information and guessing entropy both are. For nested
grouping (netlist ⊃ session ⊃ trace) use the **multi-level bootstrap**:
resample netlists, then sessions within the chosen netlists.

Report \(G\) next to every interval. A 95% CI from \(G=4\) sessions and
\(N=10^6\) traces is driven entirely by those 4 sessions, and \(N\) is
decoration. **Plan for \(G\ge 30\) sessions** so the interval means something
(master plan Stage 2 already requires 30 seeds; this extends it to sessions).

### 8.4 Permutation tests

Distribution-free, and the right tool when the statistic has no clean null
distribution (correlation after band selection, MI, classifier accuracy).

Permute the **label within session** (or within group, to respect structure),
recompute the statistic \(B\) times, and use the add-one-corrected p-value

\[
\hat p=\frac{1+\#\{b: T^{*b}\ge T_{\mathrm{obs}}\}}{B+1}.
\]

The \(+1\) is not cosmetic: it keeps \(\hat p>0\) and keeps the test valid at
finite \(B\). With \(B=10^4\) the smallest reportable p-value is \(10^{-4}\);
if a smaller one is needed, say so and use a parametric approximation with its
assumptions stated, rather than reporting \(p=0\).

Permutation also gives the **null calibration** of a biased estimator (§8.6):
the mean of \(\{T^{*b}\}\) is an estimate of the estimator's bias under
independence.

### 8.5 Multiple comparisons

Let \(m\) be the number of tests in the declared family (e.g. 50 windows × 16
key bytes × 5 bands = 4000). Sort p-values \(p_{(1)}\le\cdots\le p_{(m)}\).

- **Holm–Bonferroni** (controls family-wise error rate, use for the primary
  confirmatory claim): reject \(p_{(i)}\) while
  \(p_{(i)}\le \alpha/(m-i+1)\).
- **Benjamini–Hochberg** (controls false discovery rate at \(q\), use for
  exploratory leakage mapping): find the largest \(i\) with
  \(p_{(i)}\le \dfrac{i}{m}q\) and reject all \(j\le i\).
- **Max-statistic permutation** (best power when tests are correlated, and
  adjacent time samples are *heavily* correlated): build the null of
  \(\max_n |t_n|\) across permutations and compare the observed max to its
  quantiles. This adapts to the true correlation instead of assuming
  independence, and for time-sample scans it is usually the right choice.

Declare the family **before** looking, and record \(m\) in the results JSON.
Reducing 2000 samples to ~50 windows (§7.2) is a legitimate and large power win
precisely because it shrinks \(m\). But the windowing must be declared first,
not chosen after seeing where the peak is.

### 8.6 Mutual information with bias calibration

For discrete secret \(S\) (e.g. \(\mathrm{HW}\in\{0..8\}\)) and discretized
feature \(Z\) with \(K\) bins,

\[
\hat I_{\mathrm{plug}}(S;Z)=\sum_{s,z}\hat p(s,z)
\log_2\frac{\hat p(s,z)}{\hat p(s)\hat p(z)} .
\]

The plug-in estimator is **biased upward**; for \(N\) samples the leading term
is

\[
\mathbb E[\hat I_{\mathrm{plug}}]-I
\;\approx\;\frac{(|\mathcal S|-1)(K-1)}{2N\ln 2}\ \text{bits}.
\]

So with \(|\mathcal S|=9\), \(K=32\), \(N=10^4\): bias \(\approx 0.018\) bits,
the same order as the effects we hope to measure. **This is a primary reason the
project needs \(10^5\)–\(10^6\) traces, not a nice-to-have.** At \(N=10^6\) the
bias drops to \(1.8\times10^{-4}\) bits.

Mitigations, all three applied:

1. **Miller–Madow correction**: subtract the analytic bias above.
2. **Shuffle null**: estimate \(\hat I\) on label-permuted data; its mean is the
   empirical bias floor. Report \(\hat I - \overline{\hat I}_{\text{shuffle}}\)
   and the shuffle distribution's 95th percentile as a detection threshold.
   This is the most trustworthy of the three because it needs no asymptotics.
3. **Sample-size sweep**: plot \(\hat I\) versus \(N\) on a \(1/N\) axis and
   extrapolate to \(1/N\to0\). A straight line confirms the \(O(1/N)\) bias
   model; curvature means the binning is too fine.

For continuous features prefer the **KSG \(k\)-NN estimator** (\(k=3..5\)) or a
Gaussian-copula bound, and *always* run the same shuffle null. For a Gaussian
reference check,

\[
I_{\mathrm{Gauss}}=\tfrac12\log_2\!\left(1+\mathrm{SNR}\right),
\qquad
\mathrm{SNR}=\frac{\mathrm{Var}(\mathbb E[Z\mid S])}
{\mathbb E[\mathrm{Var}(Z\mid S)]},
\]

which is the sanity anchor: synthetic Gaussian data with known SNR must return
\(I_{\mathrm{Gauss}}\) within the estimator's interval, or the estimator
implementation is wrong. Build this as a unit test.

Binning: declare bin edges from the **calibration split only**, then apply them
frozen to the test split. Choosing bins on test data is a subtle but real leak.

### 8.7 Bayesian hierarchical model (the centerpiece)

This is the method that makes thousands of sessions across netlists and corners
into one coherent statement with honest uncertainty, instead of 30 separate
t-tests.

Index: trace \(i\), session \(j\), netlist \(\kappa\), PVT corner \(c\),
defense \(d\). Let \(z_{ij}\) be the standardized primary feature and \(s_{ij}\)
the (centered) label.

\[
\begin{aligned}
z_{ij} &\sim \mathcal N\!\left(\mu_{j} + \delta_{j}\,s_{ij},\ \sigma^2_{j}\right)\\
\delta_{j} &\sim \mathcal N\!\left(\delta_{d(j)} + u_{\kappa(j)} + v_{c(j)},\ \tau^2\right)\\
\delta_{d} &\sim \mathcal N(0,\ 1)\qquad\text{(defense-level effect, the estimand)}\\
u_{\kappa} &\sim \mathcal N(0,\ \tau_{\mathrm{net}}^2),\qquad
v_{c}\sim \mathcal N(0,\ \tau_{\mathrm{pvt}}^2)\\
\sigma_j &\sim \text{Half-Normal}(1),\quad
\tau,\tau_{\mathrm{net}},\tau_{\mathrm{pvt}}\sim\text{Half-Normal}(0.5)
\end{aligned}
\tag{8.1}
\]

Interpretation, in words: each session has its own leakage slope \(\delta_j\);
those slopes are drawn around a defense-level mean \(\delta_d\), shifted by
netlist and corner offsets. The estimand is \(\delta_{D0}-\delta_{D3}\), and the
variance components \(\tau^2,\tau^2_{\mathrm{net}},\tau^2_{\mathrm{pvt}}\)
answer H6 directly: if \(\tau_{\mathrm{net}}\) is large, "the leakage of D3" is
not a single number and must be reported as a distribution.

Why this and not a t-test:

- **Partial pooling**: sessions with few traces are shrunk toward the group
  mean, which is both more accurate and more honest than either pooling
  everything (ignores heterogeneity) or treating each session separately
  (overfits small sessions).
- **Variance decomposition**: it separates trace noise, session noise, netlist
  effect, and corner effect. A frequentist cluster bootstrap gives the right
  interval but not this decomposition.
- **Posterior over the estimand**: \(\Pr(\delta_{D0}-\delta_{D3}>0\mid\text{data})\)
  is directly interpretable, and the posterior samples feed §11 without
  reinterpretation.

Priors are weakly informative on standardized data; the half-normal scales say
"effects of more than a few standard deviations are implausible," which is true
and cheap to defend. **Run a prior predictive check** (simulate from the priors,
confirm the implied traces are physically plausible) and a **simulation-based
calibration** pass (fit the model to data simulated from the model and confirm
rank-uniformity of the true parameter in the posterior). SBC is the test that
catches a mis-specified or mis-coded hierarchical model, and it is worth the day
it takes.

Implementation: NumPyro or PyMC with NUTS; 4 chains, check \(\hat R<1.01\) and
effective sample size \(>400\) per parameter. For \(10^6\) traces, **fit on
per-session sufficient statistics** \((\hat\delta_j, \mathrm{SE}_j, n_j)\) from
a per-session OLS rather than on raw traces. The two-stage (measurement-error /
meta-analytic) formulation is statistically equivalent for the Gaussian case
and turns an intractable fit into a seconds-long one. This is the key scaling
trick for the whole hierarchical analysis.

Always report the frequentist cluster bootstrap (§8.3) next to the posterior. If
they disagree materially, the model is wrong or the bootstrap grouping is wrong;
either way, do not publish until that is resolved.

### 8.8 GLM / logistic baseline

Before any machine learning, fit an interpretable model. For the binary
distinguishing task (TVLA group membership) or a bit-level key hypothesis,

\[
\log\frac{\Pr(G=1\mid \mathbf z)}{\Pr(G=0\mid \mathbf z)}
=\beta_0+\boldsymbol\beta^\top \mathbf z ,
\]

fit by maximum likelihood on the train split, evaluated on test. Compare nested
models with a likelihood-ratio test,

\[
\Lambda=2\left(\ell_{\text{full}}-\ell_{\text{reduced}}\right)
\sim\chi^2_{\Delta p}\quad\text{under }H_0 ,
\]

and report AUC with a cluster-bootstrap CI. For the HW label, a Poisson or
ordinal GLM is the natural choice.

The GLM is the baseline that an ML result must beat by a *stated margin with a
CI* to be worth including. Coefficients \(\beta\) localized to specific windows
or bands are directly interpretable as "this cycle/band carries the leakage,"
which a neural network will not tell us.

### 8.9 Attack-side evaluation metrics

These evaluate the *observer*, i.e. they populate the payoff matrix.

- **CPA correlation** for key hypothesis \(k\):
  \(\hat\rho_k=\mathrm{corr}\big(\mathrm{HW}(\mathrm{Sbox}(X\oplus k)),\,z\big)\).
- **Guessing entropy** over \(R\) independent attack repetitions:
  \[
  \mathrm{GE}(n)=\frac{1}{R}\sum_{r=1}^{R}\mathrm{rank}_r(k^\star; n),
  \]
  where \(\mathrm{rank}_r\) is the position of the true key in the \(r\)-th
  run's score vector using \(n\) traces. Report \(\mathrm{GE}(n)\) as a curve
  with a bootstrap band over \(r\), not a single number. **Repetitions must use
  disjoint trace groups**, or \(R\) is fake.
- **Success rate** \(\mathrm{SR}(n)=\frac1R\sum_r\mathbf1[\mathrm{rank}_r=1]\),
  with a Wilson or Jeffreys interval (not the normal approximation, which
  misbehaves near 0 and 1, exactly where these results live).
- **Traces-to-disclosure**: smallest \(n\) with \(\mathrm{GE}(n)\le 1.5\),
  reported with the CI from the GE band. It is a derived quantity and inherits
  all the uncertainty of the curve.

### 8.10 Optional ML, strictly as an attack baseline

A small CNN or MLP on windowed traces may serve as a *stronger adaptive
observer* (observer type \(\theta=\) "resource-rich"). Conditions:

1. It is never the contribution; it is a baseline that stresses the defense.
2. It is compared against CPA and the GLM on identical splits.
3. Group-aware splits (§6.5). Otherwise the reported accuracy is session
   fingerprinting, which is the standard way deep-learning side-channel results
   turn out to be wrong.
4. Hyperparameters are tuned on the calibration split only, with the budget
   recorded.
5. Reported with a cluster-bootstrap CI over sessions, plus a permutation null.
6. No deep RL anywhere (master plan / roadmap §8).

## 9. Sample size and power planning

Do this **before** generating P2/P3, and record the numbers in the plan so the
final \(n\) is not a post-hoc choice.

### 9.1 Two-group mean difference

For a two-sided test at level \(\alpha\) with power \(1-\beta\) and
standardized effect \(d\), per-group size is

\[
\boxed{\;n=\frac{2\left(z_{1-\alpha/2}+z_{1-\beta}\right)^2}{d^2}\;}
\tag{9.1}
\]

With \(\alpha=0.05\), \(1-\beta=0.8\): \((1.96+0.84)^2=7.85\), so
\(n\approx 15.7/d^2\).

| \(d\) | \(n\) per group (ind.) | with \(\mathrm{DEFF}=5\) |
|---|---|---|
| 0.5 | 63 | 315 |
| 0.1 | 1 570 | 7 850 |
| 0.05 | 6 280 | 31 400 |
| 0.02 | 39 250 | 196 250 |
| 0.01 | 157 000 | 785 000 |

If the family is \(m\) tests with Bonferroni/Holm, replace \(\alpha\) by
\(\alpha/m\); for \(m=4000\), \(z_{1-\alpha/2m}\approx 4.3\), giving
\(n\approx 26.5/d^2\), a ~1.7× inflation, which is the explicit price of the
scan and should be budgeted rather than discovered.

### 9.2 Design effect: the correction that actually determines \(n\)

Clustered data inflates the needed \(n\) by the **design effect**

\[
\mathrm{DEFF}=1+(\bar n_{\text{clust}}-1)\,\mathrm{ICC},
\qquad
\mathrm{ICC}=\frac{\tau^2}{\tau^2+\sigma^2},
\]

with \(\tau^2\) the between-session variance from (8.1). With 1000 traces per
session and a *tiny* \(\mathrm{ICC}=0.004\), \(\mathrm{DEFF}=1+999(0.004)\approx
5\): **five times** as many traces for the same power. With \(\mathrm{ICC}=0.05\),
\(\mathrm{DEFF}\approx 51\).

The operational consequence is the single most important sizing lesson in this
document: **more sessions beat more traces per session.** Effective sample size
saturates at roughly \(G/\mathrm{ICC}\) no matter how many traces each session
contains. Estimate ICC from P1, then size P3 as "many sessions, moderate traces
each" rather than "few huge sessions." Budget \(G\ge 30\), ideally \(G\ge 100\),
across \(\ge 5\) netlists and \(\ge 3\) PVT corners.

### 9.3 Mutual information resolution

To resolve an MI difference \(\Delta I\), two requirements:

1. **Bias below the effect**: \(\dfrac{(|\mathcal S|-1)(K-1)}{2N\ln2}\ll\Delta I\).
   For \(\Delta I=0.01\) bits, \(|\mathcal S|=9\), \(K=32\):
   \(N\gg 1.8\times10^4\); a 10× margin gives \(N\approx 2\times 10^5\).
2. **Variance below the effect**: the shuffle-null standard deviation scales
   roughly as \(1/\sqrt N\); calibrate it empirically at P1 and extrapolate.

This is the quantitative justification for the \(10^5\)–\(10^6\) target, and it
should be stated that way in the paper: the scale is *required by the estimator*,
not chosen for impressiveness.

### 9.4 Correlation and equivalence

Fisher-\(z\) for a target correlation \(\rho\):
\(z=\operatorname{artanh}(\rho)\), \(\mathrm{SE}=1/\sqrt{n-3}\), so

\[
n \approx \left(\frac{z_{1-\alpha/2}+z_{1-\beta}}
{\operatorname{artanh}\rho}\right)^2+3 .
\]

For H4 (behavioral vs. gate-level *agreement*) the goal is to **fail to reject
nothing**: a non-significant difference is not evidence of agreement. Use
**TOST equivalence**: declare a margin \(\Delta_{\mathrm{eq}}\) (e.g. the
ordering must agree within 0.05 bits of MI), and reject non-equivalence only if
the 90% CI for the difference lies entirely within
\([-\Delta_{\mathrm{eq}},+\Delta_{\mathrm{eq}}]\). The margin must be declared
before P4 runs, and justified on engineering grounds (what difference would
change a design decision), not chosen to make the result come out.

### 9.5 Compute budget

Per-trace cost at the behavioral level: ~1–20 ms (Verilator) including feature
extraction. Then

\[
T_{\text{wall}}\approx \frac{N\cdot t_{\text{trace}}}{N_{\text{cores}}} .
\]

\(10^6\) traces at 10 ms on 8 cores \(\approx 3.5\) hours. Gate-level with SDF
is 50–500× slower, which is exactly why P4 is \(10^4\), not \(10^6\), and why
the behavioral-vs-gate-level equivalence check (H4) carries so much weight: it
is what licenses using the cheap level for the large-\(N\) claims.

## 10. Sequential analysis without p-hacking

Generating traces is cheap and incremental, so the temptation to "run until
significant" is structural. Three legitimate options; pick one per experiment
and record the choice in the pre-registration.

### 10.1 Fixed-\(n\) (default for confirmatory runs)

Compute \(n\) from §9, generate exactly that many, analyze once. Simple,
maximally defensible. **This is the default for every test split result.** If
the result is ambiguous, the honest move is to declare a *new* experiment with a
new \(n\), not to extend the old one.

### 10.2 Group-sequential with alpha spending

Pre-specify \(J\) interim looks at information fractions
\(t_1<\cdots<t_J=1\) with \(t_j=n_j/n_{\max}\). Use an O'Brien–Fleming-type
spending function

\[
\alpha^*(t)=2\left(1-\Phi\!\left(\frac{z_{1-\alpha/2}}{\sqrt t}\right)\right),
\]

which spends almost no alpha early (so early peeks are very hard to pass) and
nearly all of it at the final look. Boundaries are then derived so the total
type-I error equals \(\alpha\). Implement with a tested library, verify the
boundaries by simulation under \(H_0\) (generate null data with the real
grouping structure and confirm the empirical crossing rate is \(\le\alpha\)),
and **report the stopping stage** along with a bias-adjusted estimate. Naive
estimates at an early stopping boundary are biased away from zero, often
substantially.

### 10.3 Anytime-valid confidence sequences (preferred for exploration)

A confidence sequence \(\mathrm{CS}_n\) satisfies
\(\Pr\left(\exists n: \delta\notin \mathrm{CS}_n\right)\le\alpha\), so it can be
monitored continuously: look as often as you like, stop whenever you like. The
empirical-Bernstein form for a mean of \([0,1]\)-bounded observations is

\[
\mathrm{CS}_n=\hat\mu_n \pm
\sqrt{\frac{2\hat\sigma_n^2\log(\log(2n)/\alpha)}{n}}
+\frac{7\log(\log(2n)/\alpha)}{3(n-1)} ,
\]

the width shrinking as \(\sqrt{\log\log n/n}\), a modest and well-spent price
for unlimited peeking. Equivalently, track an **e-value** \(E_n\) (a
nonnegative martingale under \(H_0\)) and reject when \(E_n\ge 1/\alpha\);
Ville's inequality guarantees \(\Pr(\sup_n E_n\ge1/\alpha)\le\alpha\) regardless
of the stopping rule. E-values also compose cleanly across experiments by
multiplication, which is useful when accumulating evidence across phases.

Apply at the **session** level (one observation per session) so the
independence assumption holds.

### 10.4 Rules that apply regardless

- The test split is touched **once** per declared hypothesis. Every additional
  look is logged, counted in \(m\), and reported.
- Exploration happens on train/calibration splits and is labeled exploratory in
  the write-up. Exploratory findings generate new hypotheses for a *new* data
  generation run, not new tests on the same test split.
- Pre-register primary metric, \(n\), family size \(m\), stopping rule, and
  exclusion criteria in a committed, timestamped file before generation.
- Log every excluded trace with a reason; report the exclusion count per arm. A
  differential exclusion rate between arms is itself a finding.
- Negative results ship. A defense that does not work is a result.

## 11. Propagating uncertainty into the game equilibrium

This is where the statistics meet the theory, and it extends Target T5 of
`model-and-proof-roadmap.md`.

### 11.1 The problem

The payoff matrix \(M\in\mathbb R^{D\times A}\) is *estimated*, with
\(M_{da}=\) leakage + normalized costs for defense \(d\) against observer
\(a\). The equilibrium value \(v(M)\) and policy \(p^\star(M)\) are functions of
a random matrix. Reporting \(p^\star(\widehat M)\) without an interval is the
same error as reporting a point estimate without a CI, but with an extra
nonlinear map in between. Worse, \(p^\star\) can be *discontinuous* in \(M\) even
where \(v\) is not.

### 11.2 Simultaneous confidence set for \(M\)

With a bounded per-run statistic in \([0,1]\) and \(n_{da}\) independent
*sessions* per cell, Hoeffding plus a union bound over the \(DA\) cells gives,
with probability \(\ge 1-\delta\),

\[
\boxed{\;\|\widehat M-M\|_\infty\le
\epsilon=\sqrt{\frac{\ln(2DA/\delta)}{2\,n_{\min}}}\;}
\tag{11.1}
\]

For \(D=A=4\), \(\delta=0.05\), \(n_{\min}=30\) sessions:
\(\epsilon=\sqrt{\ln(640)/60}=0.33\), which is uselessly wide. At \(n_{\min}=1000\):
\(\epsilon=0.057\). This calculation alone justifies the large-\(N\)/large-\(G\)
program, and it should be presented early in the paper rather than buried: the
scale is what makes the equilibrium statement non-vacuous.

Note \(n_{\min}\) counts **sessions**, not traces (§9.2). Empirical Bernstein
replaces \(\sqrt{\ln(\cdot)/2n}\) with a variance-dependent bound and is much
tighter when per-cell variance is small; use it once P1 provides variance
estimates. And note the precondition from the roadmap: the per-run statistic
must actually be bounded. Raw MI estimates are not, so the bounded statistic
must be declared (e.g. clipped normalized MI, or success-rate) before this bound
is invoked.

### 11.3 Lipschitz transfer to the equilibrium value

For zero-sum matrix games, \(v(M)=\min_p\max_q p^\top Mq\) is 1-Lipschitz in
\(\|\cdot\|_\infty\):

\[
|v(M)-v(\widehat M)|\le\|M-\widehat M\|_\infty\le\epsilon .
\tag{11.2}
\]

Proof in one line: for any fixed \(p,q\),
\(|p^\top(M-\widehat M)q|\le\|M-\widehat M\|_\infty\) since \(p,q\) are
probability vectors; the min-max of two functions differing pointwise by at most
\(\epsilon\) differ by at most \(\epsilon\). (This is T5; it is elementary, and
should be stated as such.) So a simultaneous CI on \(M\) transfers directly to
an interval on \(v\), with no extra assumptions.

**The policy does not inherit this.** \(p^\star\) can jump when the support
changes. Therefore report (a) an interval for \(v\), (b) a bootstrap
distribution over \(p^\star\), and (c) **support stability**: the fraction of
bootstrap replicates in which each action is in the support. An action in the
support of 95% of replicates is a real recommendation; one at 40% is noise, and
saying so is more useful than reporting a single mixed strategy to three
decimals.

### 11.4 Bootstrap and posterior equilibrium distributions

Two complementary routes; run both and compare.

**(a) Cluster bootstrap over the equilibrium.** For \(b=1..B\): resample
sessions, recompute **all** \(DA\) cells from the resampled data, solve the LP,
store \(v^{*b}\), \(p^{\star *b}\), and the support indicator. Report percentile
intervals for \(v\) and marginal intervals plus support frequencies for
\(p^\star\). Because the same resample feeds every cell, this preserves the
correlation between cells. This matters since cells sharing a defense share
sessions, and ignoring that correlation gives the wrong interval on \(v\).

**(b) Posterior propagation.** From the hierarchical model (8.1), draw
posterior samples \(M^{(t)}\) (each a full matrix, with cells coupled through
the shared variance components), solve the LP for each, and obtain posterior
distributions over \(v\) and \(p^\star\). This yields directly interpretable
statements:

\[
\Pr\!\left(v(M) \le v_0 \mid \text{data}\right),
\qquad
\Pr\!\left(d\in\mathrm{supp}(p^\star)\mid\text{data}\right),
\qquad
\Pr\!\left(p^\star\neq p_{\text{robust}}\mid\text{data}\right).
\]

The last one is H5 answered as a probability instead of a yes/no, which is a
substantially stronger form of the project's central claim. Note the
approximation being made: this propagates *estimation* uncertainty, not model
misspecification. If (8.1) is wrong, the posterior is confidently wrong, which
is why SBC and the bootstrap cross-check in §8.7 are not optional.

Cost: \(B=2000\) LP solves on a \(4\times4\) matrix is seconds. Even \(20\times20\)
is minutes. There is no computational excuse for a point-estimate equilibrium.

### 11.5 Robust value as a function of \(\epsilon\)

Connect to Target T4 by reporting the worst-case value over the confidence set:

\[
v_{\mathrm{rob}}(\epsilon)=\min_{p\in\Delta_D}\max_{q\in\Delta_A}
\max_{\|E\|_\infty\le\epsilon} p^\top (\widehat M+E)q
= v(\widehat M)+\epsilon ,
\]

because the inner maximization is attained at \(E=\epsilon\mathbf{1}\mathbf1^\top\)
for probability vectors. The *value* shift is therefore trivial and should not be
oversold. The interesting object is how the **argmin** \(p^\star\) changes under
structured (per-cell, non-uniform) uncertainty, which requires the per-cell
\(\epsilon_{da}\) from §11.2 and is no longer a one-liner. That structured case
is the actual research content here; the uniform case is a sanity check.

## 13. Digital-EE skill map

The research output and the skill-building output are deliberately the same
work. Each row is a concrete artifact that is also a résumé-legible skill.

### 13.1 RTL microarchitecture
Design the AES datapath width (8/32/128-bit) and pipeline depth; the choice
changes *which cycle* leaks and how many cycles the trigger window spans.
Deliverable: a block diagram plus a cycle-accurate timing table for each
datapath variant, and the leakage consequence of each.

### 13.2 Clock gating
Instantiate ICG cells (or infer them and verify in the Yosys report) for
non-critical register banks. Deliverable: before/after dynamic-power numbers,
and a measurement of how randomized gating changes \(Q[k]\) statistics. Verify
that gating never violates functional correctness; this is what the assertions
in §13.5 are for.

### 13.3 Frequency hopping / DVFS controller
A small FSM that selects among \(F\) clock sources (or divider ratios) per
operation, driven by the randomness source. Deliverables: clock-domain-crossing
handling with proper synchronizers, a constraint file declaring all generated
clocks, a timing report that still closes in every mode, and the measured
spectral smearing (Fig. X3).

CDC is the part most likely to produce a silent bug. Treat a CDC review as a
required gate before any hopping result is believed.

### 13.4 Randomness source: LFSR vs. CSPRNG
- **LFSR**: an \(n\)-bit maximal-length LFSR with a primitive feedback
  polynomial has period \(2^n-1\), costs \(n\) flops and a few XORs, and is
  **linear and therefore predictable from \(2n\) output bits**.
- **CSPRNG**: e.g. a Trivium or ChaCha-based generator. It costs orders of
  magnitude more area/energy, but is not predictable from observed output.

The decision is a genuine security-engineering tradeoff and belongs in the
paper: if the observer can predict the dummy-activity sequence, the dummy
activity is *subtractable* and the defense collapses. State the threat model
explicitly (does the observer see enough to invert the LFSR?), and if an LFSR is
used, justify it under that model rather than by convenience. A measurable
experiment: give the observer the LFSR state and show the leakage reduction
vanish. That experiment is a strong result either way it comes out.

### 13.5 SystemVerilog assertions
```systemverilog
// Clock gating must never drop a cycle the datapath needed.
assert property (@(posedge clk) disable iff (!rst_n)
    (datapath_req |-> !bank_clk_gated))
  else $error("gated an active bank");

// Frequency mode must be stable across an operation.
assert property (@(posedge clk) disable iff (!rst_n)
    (start |=> $stable(freq_mode) throughout (done[->1])))
  else $error("mode changed mid-operation");

// Dummy bank must never influence the result (non-interference).
assert property (@(posedge clk) disable iff (!rst_n)
    (done |-> result == expected_result))
  else $error("dummy activity corrupted datapath");
```
The non-interference assertion is the important one: it is the formal statement
that the countermeasure does not change functionality, and it is checkable on
every one of the \(10^6\) simulation runs essentially for free. Deliverable: an
assertion coverage report showing each property was actually exercised. A
property that never fires its antecedent proves nothing.

### 13.6 Synthesis, timing, and power reports
Run Yosys → Sky130; collect cell count/area, critical path (via OpenSTA), and
power (via SAIF-annotated power analysis). These numbers populate the
\(\lambda_E,\lambda_L\) cost terms of the game with *measured* overheads,
replacing the normalized placeholders in the master plan §2. This is the single
highest-value connection between the digital track and the theory: it removes
invented constants from the objective function.

Deliverable: Table X6, plus a short note on each defense's area/timing/power cost and
whether timing still closes.

### 13.7 FPGA prototype (optional, gate R4)
Target an inexpensive owned board (iCE40/ECP5 with open tools, or an Artix-7
class board). Capture supply current with a shunt and a scope, triggered by the
same `trigger` signal as the simulation. Purpose is **calibration of the proxy**
(§0): regress measured per-cycle current against simulated \(Q[k]\) and report
\(R^2\) plus the fitted scale. That regression is the honest answer to "is your
proxy meaningful," and a low \(R^2\) is a publishable limitation, not a failure.

Stay inside the safety boundary: owned hardware, synthetic keys, cabled and
shielded.

### 13.8 Interfaces and register map
A small APB/AXI-Lite or simple parallel register interface:

| Offset | Name | Bits | Access | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0x00 | `CTRL` | [0] start, [1] soft_reset | RW | operation control |
| 0x04 | `STATUS` | [0] busy, [1] done | RO | status |
| 0x08 | `DEF_MODE` | [1:0] defense action | RW | \(d\in\{D0..D3\}\) |
| 0x0C | `DEF_PARAM` | [3:0] dummy level, [7:4] gate mask | RW | defense parameters |
| 0x10 | `PRNG_SEED` | [31:0] | RW | reproducible defense randomness |
| 0x14 | `FREQ_SET` | [3:0] enabled hop modes | RW | hop set |
| 0x18 | `CYCLE_CNT` | [31:0] | RO | measured latency |

Deliverable: the register map document, a UVM-lite or cocotb testbench that
exercises every register, and the **identical** register map used by the
simulation harness and the FPGA firmware. Identical interfaces across
simulation and hardware are what make R4 a calibration rather than a new
experiment.

## 14. Stage gates for the digital track

These interleave with the master plan's twelve stages; R1 can begin as soon as
Stage 1 is done.

### R0: Toolchain and DUT-A (1–2 sessions)
- `iverilog` + Verilator + GTKWave installed; versions recorded.
- DUT-A simulates, VCD parses, per-cycle \(Q[k]\) extracted.
- Ground-truth check: with `dummy_level=0`, correlation between toggle count and
  HD label is \(>0.95\).
- **Exit**: one trace reproducible bit-exactly from a seed on a second machine.

### R1: 10k behavioral traces and full statistics (1 week)
- \(\ge10^4\) traces, \(G\ge30\) sessions, D0–D3 × A0–A3.
- Parquet features + Zarr traces + manifest, checksums recorded.
- Group splits with the disjointness assertion passing.
- All of §8 runs: TVLA with max-\(t\) permutation, cluster bootstrap, MI with
  all three bias controls, GLM, hierarchical fit with \(\hat R<1.01\).
- ICC and DEFF estimated; P3's \(n\) and \(G\) computed from them.
- **Exit**: null calibration passes (zero-leakage data returns no detection at
  the declared level), and the estimated effects have intervals that are not
  dominated by \(G\).

### R2: Gate-level switching (1–2 weeks)
- Yosys → Sky130 netlist; cell capacitances feed \(c_n\).
- Gate-level simulation, first zero-delay then SDF-annotated.
- \(10^4\) traces at gate level; H4 tested with a pre-declared TOST margin.
- Synthesis/timing/power reports → Table X6.
- **Exit**: behavioral and gate-level orderings of \(\mathcal D\) either agree
  within the margin (behavioral is licensed for scale), or disagree and the
  disagreement is characterized and reported.

### R3: Scale to 100k–1M (1–2 weeks, mostly compute)
- Verilator fast path, parallel workers, chunked Zarr writes.
- \(10^5\) first; \(10^6\) only if R3's \(10^5\) analysis is clean.
- MI-vs-\(1/N\) convergence demonstrated (Fig. X4).
- Payoff matrix with per-cell CIs; bootstrap and posterior equilibria (§11).
- **Exit**: equilibrium value interval from (11.1)–(11.2) is narrow enough to
  separate at least two defense actions; otherwise report that it is not and say
  what \(G\) would be required.

### R4: FPGA calibration (optional, gated)
Enter only if R1–R3 predict an effect larger than expected instrument noise
(master plan Stage 7 criteria) and a board is already owned or cheap.
- **Exit**: measured-vs-simulated activity regression with \(R^2\) and CI, and
  an explicit statement of how much of the simulation conclusion survives.

## 15. Build/buy and infrastructure

**Buy/use free (do not build):** simulators, synthesis tools, PDK, storage
libraries, statistical samplers. All of these are free and better than anything
we would write.

**Build:** the DUTs, the defense wrapper, the testbench, the VCD→feature
extractor, the channel model, the dataset generator, the analysis package. These
are the research artifact.

**Do not build:** a custom simulator, a custom statistical sampler, a custom
storage format, or a neural architecture.

**Compute:** a laptop/desktop with 8+ cores, 16+ GB RAM, and 100 GB free SSD is
sufficient through R3 at \(10^6\) behavioral traces. Gate-level SDF at \(10^4\)
is the slow step. Consider a short cloud burst *only* if R2 proves to be the
bottleneck, and compute the cost first. This is a case where an afternoon of
Verilator optimization usually beats renting machines.

**Storage discipline:** keep raw VCDs *nowhere*. Keep features, traces,
manifests, and seeds. The manifest plus the generator commit plus the master
seed must be sufficient to regenerate everything; make that a tested property by
regenerating a 1000-trace subset in CI and comparing checksums.

**Versioning:** dataset version is semver in the manifest and in every Parquet
file. Never mutate a published dataset; publish `v2`.

## 16. Threats to validity, stated up front

1. **Proxy validity.** VCD activity is not EM leakage (§0). Unmitigated until
   R4, and only partially even then.
2. **Channel-model validity.** \(h_{\mathrm{PDN}}\), the pulse shape \(g\), and
   the RF mixing model are declared assumptions. Sensitivity analysis over them
   is mandatory; conclusions that flip under a plausible alternative channel are
   not conclusions.
3. **No glitch power at behavioral level.** Addressed by R2/H4, and the result
   of H4 must be reported whichever way it comes out.
4. **Simulation monoculture.** All traces share one generator; a bug is a
   systematic error that no amount of \(N\) will reveal. Mitigate with the
   Icarus-vs-Verilator cross-check, the ground-truth DUT-A check, and the
   null-calibration test.
5. **\(N\) is not \(G\).** A million traces from three sessions is three
   observations. Guarded by §8.3 and §9.2, and by reporting \(G\) everywhere.
6. **Estimator bias.** MI, GE, and classifier accuracy are all biased in the
   optimistic direction; guarded by shuffle nulls and group splits.
7. **Not novel.** Pre-silicon leakage simulation, gate-level power side-channel
   estimation, and RTL leakage assessment are established fields, as is the use
   of large synthetic datasets. The contribution hypothesis is the *game-theoretic
   use* of such a dataset with propagated uncertainty, not the dataset. Query
   Group F in `literature/novelty-search-log.md` (placeholder) covers: pre-silicon /
   RTL / gate-level leakage simulation and assessment, synthetic side-channel
   dataset generation, FPGA power side-channel datasets (e.g. ASCAD-style and
   CHES-contest datasets), and statistical methodology for large trace sets.
   Until that search is logged, make no comparative claim about dataset scale.
